Principles

  1. Built, not imagined: real artefacts instead of robots, brains and glow.
  2. Stand out from the feed: colours that differ from LinkedIn's own in light and dark mode.
  3. One idea, one glance: one point per slide, made visible with a mini chart where it helps.
  4. Face first: the author is visible on every piece.
  5. Excited, not hyped: energy from colour, scale and contrast, never hype words or emoji.

Typography

One family, Onest (Google Fonts, SIL Open Font License, free for commercial use), at two weights: 400 and 900. No monospace; numbers are set in Onest.

Rules

  • Nothing smaller than 32px on a slide. Phones show a 1080px slide at about a third of its size.
  • Two weights only. 900 for headlines, stats and names; 400 for everything else. Emphasis inside body uses deep coral or the butter highlight, not a third weight.
  • Headlines: line height 1.05, letter-spacing -1.5%. Body: 1.45, no tracking. Tight enough to feel confident, open enough to stay friendly.
  • Sentence case everywhere, no full stops on headlines. Reads like a person talking, not a template.
  • Body never runs past three lines on a slide. One idea, one glance.
  • Step slides lead with a big deep-coral numeral. It brings the signature onto content slides and passes contrast (4.6:1).

Rules

  • Covers use a pastel background. Only the cover shows in the feed, and LinkedIn's feed is warm off-white (#F4F2EE) or neutral dark (#1B1F23), so a pastel cover stands out in both.
  • Pastels are fills, never text. Use ink on them, and deep coral when a word needs colour.
  • One ink-outlined card per slide. The outline marks the single idea that matters.
  • Charts: one coral value, everything else paper grey.
  • Good versus bad without red and green. "Before" in a muted hairline card; "after" in an ink-outlined card with butter on the winning phrase.
  • No dark slides on LinkedIn. Revisit for a website or talks.
